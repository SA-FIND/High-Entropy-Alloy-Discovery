How it works

The prediction starts from Maresca and Curtin's edge-dislocation theory, which has no fitted parameters. I added a correction trained on 277 measured yield strengths of 72 single-phase BCC alloys, tested from −269 to 1600 °C (open MPEA dataset, Borg et al. 2020). On alloys first reported after the training data, the model gets R² = 0.65. The theory alone gets 0.10. The ranges under each number are 90% conformal intervals; on alloys the model had not seen, they held 84 to 88% of the measurements.

It only knows Ti, Zr, Hf, V, Nb, Ta, Cr, Mo and W, and it assumes the alloy stays single-phase BCC. A warning appears when your composition is far from what has been measured. Ti-Zr-V alloys lose most of their strength between 600 and 1000 °C (NbTiVZr drops from 834 to 58 MPa), and the model can miss that, so read the 1000 °C number for those alloys with care.

What comes next

Next, I want to make one of the Ti-Zr-Hf-V-Mo candidates and test it in compression between 600 and 1000 °C. After that, I want to extend the model to corrosion-resistant and lightweight high-entropy alloys.
